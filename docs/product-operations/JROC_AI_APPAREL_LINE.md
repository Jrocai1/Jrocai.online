## Manufacturing routes

1. **Private-label blank garment:** qualify the brand's permission to relabel and decorate an existing garment; verify the blank's exact fabric, fit, quality, origin and documentation.
2. **Custom cut-and-sew:** develop a JROC-AI-controlled garment technical pack and contract a qualified factory to manufacture to the approved specification.
3. **Decoration and finishing:** use an approved print, embroidery, woven label, heat-transfer or other decoration supplier only after artwork, placement, durability and mark permissions are approved.

Supplier must disclose whether it is the factory, an authorized agent, wholesaler or decorator. Do not claim JROC-AI designed or manufactured a garment when the actual relationship is private label or contract manufacturing.

## Technical pack requirements

Each style and revision needs:
- Style name, unique style code, intended wearer and age grade.
- Approved front, back, side and detail views or technical flats.
- Fit and silhouette description, once selected.
- Size chart, points of measure, grading rules and measurement tolerances.
- Fabric construction, verified fiber content by percentage, fabric weight and finish.
- Color standard and approved lab dip or physical swatch.
- Stitch type, seam construction, thread and reinforcement details.
- Trims, drawcords, zippers, buttons, elastics and hardware specifications.
- Brand label, care label, country-of-origin and responsible-business identification layout.
- Official J-ROC AI logo artwork, exact approved version, placement, dimensions and decoration method.
- Print or embroidery color references, backing, stitch density or print process where relevant.
- Packaging, folding, size stickers, barcodes and carton markings.
- Bill of materials, factory, country of origin, revision history and sample approvals.

## Sample and quality workflow

1. Verify the supplier and manufacturing location.
2. Request fabric swatches, blank-garment samples or a development sample only after approval.
3. Check material composition evidence, fabric weight and finish.
4. Measure the garment against the approved size specification.
5. Conduct fit reviews on appropriately selected wearers and sizes.
6. Review seams, stitching, trims, closures, labels and overall workmanship.
7. Assess print or embroidery placement, durability and appearance after care testing.
8. Test dimensional change after laundering and colorfastness using a suitable test protocol.
9. Approve a pre-production sample as the retained reference sample.
10. Inspect the production batch against the approved reference before stock is released.

Record defects by type, severity, quantity, batch and corrective action. Do not silently accept substitutions in fabric, dye lot, trim, decoration process or factory.

## US apparel labeling and compliance checklist

Before selling each applicable garment, obtain qualified review of:
- Fiber-content declarations and percentages.
- Country-of-origin disclosures.
- Responsible business identity, using the proper company name or a valid Federal Trade Commission registered identification number where appropriate.
- Care instructions.
- Applicable clothing-textile flammability requirements.
- Extra requirements for children's apparel, sleepwear, accessories, drawstrings or special materials where relevant.
- Importer records, claims and advertising accuracy.
- Any applicable packaging, tracking, chemical, state or other market requirements.

Useful official references:
- Federal Trade Commission apparel labeling: https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/topics/tools-consumers/apparel-labeling
- Textile Fiber Rule, 16 C.F.R. Part 303: https://www.ftc.gov/legal-library/browse/rules/textile-fiber-rule
- FTC textile and wool labeling guidance: https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/threading-your-way-through-labeling-requirements-under-textile-wool-acts
- CPSC clothing flammability guidance: https://www.cpsc.gov/FAQ/Clothing

These references are starting points. A product-specific review is still required; this document is not a legal determination.

## Apparel costing and pricing

Calculate true landed cost per sellable unit:
- Garment or cut-and-sew cost.
- Decoration, labels and trims.
- Packaging and labeling.
- Freight, duties, customs and inspection.
- Fulfillment and payment fees.
- Returns, exchanges, defects and warranty allowance.
- Any marketplace or sales tax obligations handled by the business.

Set retail pricing only after actual supplier quotes and unit economics are available. Do not publish invented prices, stock counts, customer reviews or delivery dates.

## Apparel release gate

A garment remains Draft / Research until:
- Brand artwork and garment design are approved.
- The technical pack and bill of materials are complete.
- Supplier, factory and private-label rights are verified.
- Required label and compliance review is complete.
- Samples and production inspection pass.
- Size and variant SKUs are controlled.
- Care, returns and shipping information is ready.
- Actual product photography matches the released garment.
- The Chairman approves the exact garment, color, size range, price and quantity for sale.
